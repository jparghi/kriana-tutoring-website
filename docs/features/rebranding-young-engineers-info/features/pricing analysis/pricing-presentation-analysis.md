# Pricing Presentation Analysis

## Context

On August 31, 2026, a prospective customer said the robotics packages looked too expensive. The current Smartivo booking page presents two complete learning paths:

| Path | Classes | Pay in full | Installment option | Program rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Builder | 20 | $494 promotional subtotal ($520 normally) | Up to 4 payments; $560 subtotal | $26/class paid in full; $28/class by installments |
| Engineer | 36 | $840 promotional subtotal ($864 normally) | Up to 6 payments; $1,008 subtotal | $24/class paid in full; $28/class by installments |

Applicable tax is additional. The promotion gives the first class free only on the pay-in-full option.

The prices themselves are not necessarily the problem. The page makes the largest number the strongest visual element, so a parent first experiences a $494 or $840 purchase rather than an affordable class or payment. The parent must do the work of translating that total into 20 or 36 learning experiences.

## Recommendation

Use a **payment-first, total-always-disclosed** presentation.

The card should lead with the smallest real payment a family may choose today, not with the full package subtotal. The complete commitment and total must remain beside or immediately below it. This reduces sticker shock without creating a misleading subscription impression.

Recommended hierarchy for the current offers:

### Builder card

**20-class Builder Path**  
**4 payments of $140** + tax  
or **$494 paid in full** + tax  
Save $66 when paid in full during the offer  
Complete 20-class package · installment total $560  
First class free with pay-in-full registration by September 13

CTA: **Choose Builder**

### Engineer card

**36-class Engineer Path**  
**6 payments of $168** + tax  
or **$840 paid in full** + tax  
Save $168 when paid in full during the offer  
Complete 36-class package · installment total $1,008  
First class free with pay-in-full registration by September 13

CTA: **Choose Engineer**

The savings figures compare the installment-plan subtotal with the promotional pay-in-full subtotal. If that comparison feels too aggressive, use “Best pay-in-full price” instead and omit the savings line.

## Why this is stronger

1. **It answers the affordability question first.** “4 payments of $140” is easier to process than “$494,” even though the total obligation is clearly stated.
2. **It connects price to delivery.** “20-class path” and “36-class path” explain why the totals are larger than a single activity fee.
3. **It keeps the best economic option visible.** The paid-in-full price remains attractive, but it no longer blocks a family that cannot make one large payment.
4. **It avoids deceptive drip pricing.** Taxes and total commitments are disclosed where the payment amount appears.

## Treatment of digital lessons, materials, and other inclusions

Do not assign separate prices to standard items that are already included in the program. Present them as value, not as extra charges:

> Everything needed for the learning path is included: instructor-led classes, use of robotics materials during class, curriculum activities, and digital learning resources where applicable. No separate curriculum or digital-lesson fee.

Only use this copy after confirming every listed inclusion is accurate. If a physical kit, replacement part, take-home material, or other item is optional, show it later as an optional add-on. If it is mandatory, include it in the advertised total rather than revealing it late in checkout.

The electrician analogy is useful for understanding psychology but should not be copied literally. It is reasonable not to foreground tax or optional add-ons; it is not reasonable to hide a mandatory charge. On this page, “+ applicable tax” can be visually secondary, but it must remain close to every advertised payment and total.

## Page-level changes

Replace the current introduction:

> Totals shown are the full package subtotal, not a monthly price, plus applicable taxes.

with:

> Choose the complete learning path that fits your child. Pay in full for the best price or split the regular package total into easy payments. Prices shown before applicable tax.

Then make these visual changes:

- Make the installment amount the largest number on each card.
- Put “4 payments” or “6 payments” directly in the large-price line; do not write only “from $140.”
- Place “Complete package total: $560/$1,008” immediately below the installment price.
- Show “or $494/$840 paid in full” as the second pricing choice, visually highlighted as the best price.
- Keep the class count in the card title, not in faint supporting text.
- Add a short “What’s included” row before the CTA.
- Keep “+ tax” beside both the installment amount and paid-in-full amount.
- Do not call the installments a monthly membership. They are scheduled payments toward a fixed package commitment.

## Important pricing observation

The current installment price is materially higher than the pay-in-full price:

| Path | Promotional pay in full | Installment total | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Builder | $494 | $560 | $66 (13.4% more) |
| Engineer | $840 | $1,008 | $168 (20.0% more) |

This creates an affordability penalty: the customer who most needs smaller payments pays the most. The Engineer difference is especially large. Before changing the website, consider whether installments should divide the same package price, or carry only a modest administrative premium. A simpler offer such as “4 × $130 = $520” and “6 × $144 = $864,” with the first-class-free promotion reserved for pay in full, may feel fairer while preserving a reason to pay upfront.

## Sales fallback

The existing 10-class Explorer package at $300 is intentionally hidden from the public package grid. Keep it as a save-the-sale option rather than adding a third public card immediately:

> I understand that a 20-class commitment may feel too large right now. We also have a 10-class Explorer starting path at $300 if you would prefer a smaller first commitment.

Use this only after the family objects to the 20-class option. This preserves the main program positioning while preventing a price-sensitive lead from leaving with no suitable choice.

## Suggested phone response

> I understand—the full total can look large because it covers 20 classes, not one month. You do not have to pay it all at once. Builder can be split into four payments of $140 before tax, or it is $494 before tax if paid in full during the offer. That works out to $26 per class at the paid-in-full price. If 20 classes is still too much of a first commitment, I can also offer a 10-class Explorer path for $300.

## Test plan

Run the new presentation for enough qualified traffic to compare it with the current version. Track:

- package-card-to-registration conversion;
- Builder versus Engineer selection;
- pay-in-full versus installment selection;
- checkout completion;
- inquiries mentioning affordability;
- use of the Explorer fallback;
- refunds or confusion about the complete-package commitment.

The primary success measure should be completed paid registrations, not clicks. If card clicks rise but checkout abandonment also rises, the payment-first presentation is not disclosing the commitment clearly enough.

## Decision summary

The recommended first change is not a price cut. Reframe the same offer around **$140 per payment for Builder** and **$168 per payment for Engineer**, disclose the complete totals immediately, explain what is included, and keep the lower 10-class path as a sales fallback. Separately review whether the 13–20% installment premium is making the flexible option unnecessarily difficult to sell.

## Follow-up: monthly pricing analysis

After reviewing the payment-first proposal, the package still looks expensive because $140 and $168 are arbitrary installment amounts rather than a familiar household budget. Moving to a genuine monthly presentation is reasonable and better aligned with how other children's coding and robotics providers sell ongoing learning.

### How other providers present it

The following public prices were reviewed on August 31, 2026. Programs are not identical, so these are positioning benchmarks rather than strict like-for-like comparisons.

| Provider | Public presentation | Advertised price |
|---|---|---:|
| [CodeKidz Pickering](https://codekidz.ca/) | 1 class/week; 4 two-hour classes per month | From $200/month |
| [Zebra Robotics, Coquitlam](https://www.zebrarobotics.com/coquitlam/programs/41) | 4 one-hour robotics classes | $195/month + tax |
| [Zebra Robotics, Surrey](https://zebrarobotics.com/surrey/programs/21) | 4 one-hour robotics classes | $180/month + tax |
| [Kids Coding Online](https://kidscodingonline.com/) | 1 small-group online class/week; cancel anytime | $160/month |
| [SC4K](https://www.sc4k.ca/paymentforcanadians) | Monthly robotics plan with a stated 3- or 6-month validity period | $80–$100/month plus a $50 admission fee |
| [Ottawa Robotics Club](https://www.ottawaroboticsclub.com/jk.html) | Traditional fixed course rather than membership | $280 + HST for 10 lessons |

The important pattern is that ongoing academies foreground **one monthly number and the number of classes included**. Traditional clubs and municipal programs are more likely to foreground a complete session price.

Kriana's current regular installment rate is $28 per class. Four classes therefore cost only **$112 per four-class month**, which is below the $160–$200/month positioning used by several Canadian coding and robotics providers reviewed above. The offer currently appears expensive because the website foregrounds the total, not because the class rate is unusually high.

### Recommended model: fixed-term monthly enrollment

Use one simple monthly price for both paths:

> **Robotics classes — $112 every 4 weeks**  
> Includes 4 instructor-led classes  
> Choose a 20-class or 36-class learning path  
> Prices before applicable tax

Path details:

| Path | Billing | Regular total | Pay-in-full offer | Pay-in-full saving |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Builder | 5 payments of $112 every 4 weeks | $560 | $494 | $66 |
| Engineer | 9 payments of $112 every 4 weeks | $1,008 | $840 | $168 |

This is the cleanest conversion of the existing installment economics: no price reduction and no confusing difference between Builder and Engineer monthly payments. Engineer is the longer path, not the larger recurring bill.

Use **“every 4 weeks”** rather than “per calendar month” if each payment always covers exactly four classes. Calendar months sometimes contain five class days and scheduled holidays, so “$112/month for four classes” can create disputes about what a month includes.

### Recommended website hierarchy

Lead the page with:

> ## Hands-on robotics from $112 every 4 weeks
> Four instructor-led classes per payment period. Choose the learning path that fits your child.

Builder card:

> **Builder · 20 classes**  
> **$112 every 4 weeks**  
> 5 scheduled payments · $560 total + tax  
> **Best price: $494 + tax when paid in full**  
> Save $66 with the Back-to-School offer

Engineer card:

> **Engineer · 36 classes**  
> **$112 every 4 weeks**  
> 9 scheduled payments · $1,008 total + tax  
> **Best price: $840 + tax when paid in full**  
> Save $168 with the Back-to-School offer

This presentation makes the first number a family evaluates $112, while the payment count and full commitment remain clear.

### Do not call it “cancel anytime” unless the business model changes

There are two genuinely different monthly models:

1. **Monthly billing for a fixed learning path — recommended first.** The family commits to 20 or 36 classes and pays every four weeks. This protects curriculum continuity and revenue. The payment count and total must be disclosed before registration.
2. **Cancel-anytime membership.** The family buys four classes at a time and may leave before the next renewal. This lowers the buying barrier further, but increases churn, makes cohort planning harder, and removes the guaranteed value of a 20- or 36-class enrollment.

Do not market the first model as a “no-contract membership” or “cancel anytime.” Ontario's consumer guidance says subscription and membership contracts over $50 should provide written terms containing the purchase details and agreed credit terms: [Consumer Protection Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract). Before launch, the registration agreement should clearly state the payment frequency, number of payments, total, missed-class policy, cancellation terms, and whether payments are automatic.

### Promotion recommendation

The current “first class free” offer is easy to understand but becomes less visible against a $112 recurring price. Present it as a dollar saving:

> **Pay in full and save $66 on Builder or $168 on Engineer.**

The Engineer saving includes both its lower bulk class rate and the first-free-class promotion, so it is stronger than saying only “first class free.” Keep the promotion deadline beside this statement.

### Final monthly recommendation

Adopt **$112 every four weeks for four classes** as the primary advertised price. Keep Builder as five scheduled payments and Engineer as nine scheduled payments. Show the full total directly below the recurring price, retain the discounted pay-in-full choice, and do not promise cancel-anytime service yet.

This changes how the price is framed, not how much installment customers pay. It is also positioned below several Canadian robotics and coding providers reviewed above.
